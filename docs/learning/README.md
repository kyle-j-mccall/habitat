# Learning Journal

This is where I write down what I hit, what confused me, what clicked, and what I'd do differently. It's a notebook, not a public artifact.

## Format

One file per entry, filename `YYYY-MM-DD-short-topic.md`.

There is no template. But the entries that tend to be useful later look like this:

- **What I was trying to do** — one line.
- **What surprised me** — the moment something didn't behave the way I expected. This is the actual value; write it down while it's fresh.
- **What I learned** — the concept, pattern, or gotcha behind the surprise. Try to name the general principle, not just the specific fix.
- **What I'd do differently** — optional. Include when the answer is more nuanced than "I got it right the first time."

## Why bother

Two reasons:

1. **Interview prep.** Reviewing three months of these entries before an interview is the closest thing to a highlight reel of my own thinking. Concrete stories beat rehearsed generalities.
2. **Building intuition.** Writing about something forces me to notice what I actually understand versus what I was pattern-matching. The gap between "I got it working" and "I could explain it to someone else" is where real learning happens.

Keep entries short. A page is more than enough. If it's more than a page, it's probably really a design doc or an ADR — put it there instead.
